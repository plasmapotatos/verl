# Refusal Degradation Analysis

Analysis of why GRPO refusal training causes val-set correct answers to degrade
into IDK/not-attempted responses, and whether this varies by reward mode.

**Experiments compared:**
- `binary` — binary reward (+1 correct, -1 incorrect, 0 IDK on answer Qs)
- `ternary_adaptive` — ternary reward with group-conditional IDK promotion
- `ternary_static` — ternary reward with fixed IDK=0 on answer Qs (only pass@k from step 100)

**Evaluation set:** `train_richqa_answer_origqa_frac0.2` (val = SFT-only facts, held out of GRPO)

### `analyze_not_attempted_confidence.py` — Figs 10–13: Confidence analysis of correct→IDK
Asks: are questions that degrade (correct→IDK) low or high confidence at baseline?

**Key finding:**
- **Binary** — only 5/107 (4.7%) degrade; all had very low step-0 confidence (mean=0.14).
  These were already mostly IDK at step 0 — barely correct flukes, not genuine knowledge.
- **Ternary_adaptive** — 38/107 (35.5%) degrade; mean step-0 confidence=0.62, 12/38 were >90% correct at step 0.
  High-confidence correct answers are being converted to IDK by the ternary cascade.

**Why ternary_adaptive cascades:** The group-conditional IDK promotion (IDK→0 when group fails) creates a
feedback loop: once a question starts tipping toward IDK, the group eventually all-fails, IDK is no
longer penalized, IDK increases further, repeat.

### `analyze_incorrect_to_idk.py` — Figs 14–16: Incorrect→IDK transition analysis
Asks: what happens to questions that were *incorrect* (not correct) at baseline?
Do they also become IDK, and is that pattern similar to the correct→IDK degradation?

Classifies every val question into a trajectory type:
- `correct→IDK` — had ≥1 correct at baseline, 0 correct at step 480, IDK grew
- `correct→stay` — had correct at baseline, still correct at step 480
- `incorrect→IDK` — 0 correct, >0 incorrect at baseline, IDK fraction grew by ≥20 pp
- `incorrect→stay` — 0 correct, >0 incorrect, IDK did NOT grow
- `idk_dominated` — already ≥75% IDK at baseline

**Key finding:**
- Both binary and ternary convert confident-wrong questions to IDK in similar proportions
  (binary: 19 questions, ternary_adaptive: 42). This part is working as intended in both modes.
- The incorrect→IDK group were *highly confidently wrong* at baseline (binary: mean frac_incorrect=0.83,
  ternary: 0.87) — the model was nearly always wrong but rarely IDK. Training successfully converts
  these to IDK in both reward modes.
- So the difference between binary and ternary is NOT about the incorrect→IDK behavior —
  it's purely the correct→IDK degradation that distinguishes them.
